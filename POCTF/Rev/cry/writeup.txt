# CTF Write-Up: The Whitmore Letter

## Step 1: Identifying the Cipher

The first step was analyzing the text of the letter for cryptographic clues. The letter is addressed to **"Admiral Sir Francis Beaufort"** and explicitly references *"that reciprocal tableau which bears your name"*.

This is a direct historical reference to the **Beaufort cipher**, a substitution cipher similar to Vigenère but with a slightly modified reciprocal mechanism. In a standard Vigenère cipher, the plaintext and key are added together. In a Beaufort cipher, the ciphertext is subtracted from the key:


$$P = (K - C) \bmod 26$$

## Step 2: Extracting the Secret Key

With the cipher identified, a key was needed. The prompt instructs to *"Find the key the letter hides"*.

Looking closely at the border of the provided image (`variant_066.png`), there are several words describing plants and objects. Some of these words are marked with a red star ($\bigstar$). Reading these starred words in a clockwise direction starting from the top yields:

* **Top:** $\bigstar$ **S**wan, $\bigstar$ **U**rn


* **Right:** $\bigstar$ **N**autilus


* **Bottom:** $\bigstar$ **K**ey, $\bigstar$ **E**lder


* **Left:** $\bigstar$ **N**autilus



Taking the first letter of each starred word forms an acrostic: **S-U-N-K-E-N**.
The key is **`SUNKEN`**.

## Step 3: Decrypting the Header

Before tackling the complex payload, it is best practice to test the cipher logic on the known flag format prefix.

* **Ciphertext Prefix:** `DGLRZ`

* **Key Prefix:** `SUNKE`

Applying the Beaufort mathematical formula using A=0 to Z=25:

* **D (3):** $18 (S) - 3 = 15 \rightarrow$ **P**
* **G (6):** $20 (U) - 6 = 14 \rightarrow$ **O**
* **L (11):** $13 (N) - 11 = 2 \rightarrow$ **C**
* **R (17):** $10 (K) - 17 \equiv 19 \bmod 26 \rightarrow$ **T**
* **Z (25):** $4 (E) - 25 \equiv 5 \bmod 26 \rightarrow$ **F**

The header perfectly decrypts to **`POCTF`**, verifying both the key and the cipher method.

## Step 4: Decoding the Payload

The remaining ciphertext is: `2.417.5A6PHV52M3B2OU57.DSDD5K7VFL63SJT4E4HL5PBSL6`.

In standard CTF formats, non-alphabetic characters (numbers, punctuation, and curly braces) do not consume a position in the repeating key sequence. The key `SUNKEN` resumes right where it left off after the `POCTF{` prefix.

Since the prefix used 5 characters of the key (`SUNKE`), the payload decryption starts with the 6th letter of the key (`N`), and then repeats `SUNKEN`.

Applying the $P = (K - C) \bmod 26$ formula to the alphabetic characters only:

* A (0) with N (13) $\rightarrow 13 - 0 = 13 \rightarrow$ **N**
* P (15) with S (18) $\rightarrow 18 - 15 = 3 \rightarrow$ **D**
* H (7) with U (20) $\rightarrow 20 - 7 = 13 \rightarrow$ **N**
* V (21) with N (13) $\rightarrow 13 - 21 \equiv 18 \rightarrow$ **S**
* M (12) with K (10) $\rightarrow 10 - 12 \equiv 24 \rightarrow$ **Y**
* ... *(this process is repeated for every letter)*

Once all the letters are decrypted, we drop the intact numbers and decimal points back into their exact original positions.

## Final Flag

**`POCTF{2.417.5N6DNS52Y3D2ZY57.RVHB5D7XPC63SVU4O4NC5VDVH6}`**
